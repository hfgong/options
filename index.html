Put–Call Parity

European

A call and a put with the same strike and expiry are tied together: owning the call plus cash equal to the strike's present value pays off exactly like owning the put plus the stock.

Core C+Ke−rT=P+S
Solved C−P=S−Ke−rT
Dividends C−P=Se−qT−Ke−rT
C, P

  • Arbitrage: if C − P is above S − Ke−rT, sell the call, buy the put and the stock, and borrow the difference (a conversion); if below, do the reverse (a reversal).
  • American options (no dividends) only satisfy bounds: S − K ≤ C − P ≤ S − Ke−rT.

VIX Index

Cboe method

The market's expected volatility of the S&P 500 over the next 30 days, in annualised percent. It is computed from a strip of out-of-the-money SPX option prices, without using Black–Scholes.

Forward F=strike+eRT(call−put)
T
time to expiry in years, counted in minutes (near term T₁, next term T₂, both 23–37 days out)
K₀
first listed strike at or below the forward level F
Ki, Q(Ki)
strike of the i-th out-of-the-money option and its bid–ask midpoint (puts below K₀, calls above, both averaged at K₀)
ΔKi
half the gap between neighbouring strikes: (Ki+1 − Ki−1)/2
R
risk-free rate to each expiry (from U.S. Treasury yields)
Nx
minutes: to each expiry (NT₁, NT₂), in 30 days (43,200) and in a year (525,600)
  1. Find F at the strike where |call − put| is smallest, then set K₀.
  2. Select options: out-of-the-money puts and calls with non-zero bids, stopping after two consecutive zero bids.
  3. Compute σ₁², σ₂² for the two expiries with the per-term formula.
  4. Interpolate to a constant 30 days and annualise; VIX is 100 × the square root.
